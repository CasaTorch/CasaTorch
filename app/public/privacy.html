CasaTorch Privacy Notice

Version 2026-10-16.

The short version

CasaTorch runs on your hardware. Your accounts, settings, floor plans, photos, calendars and device data are stored there, in its data folder, and are not sent to us.

What this CasaTorch stores, on your hardware

  • Accounts: names, usernames, e-mail addresses (optional), password hashes (never the password), encrypted authenticator secrets, hashed recovery codes, and whether each person accepted these terms and when.
  • Sessions and security log: when and from which network address and browser someone signed in, signed out, failed to sign in, or changed security settings - so the owner can spot misuse. The log keeps the most recent 5,000 events.
  • Your home: device names and states, floor plans and scans, photos you upload, calendars and reminders, and the settings you choose.
  • Connections: sign-in tokens for services you connect (for example Home Assistant or Ring), and the keys or passwords for network gear you connect (UniFi, Cisco Meraki, Pi-hole, AdGuard Home), encrypted. Ring and power-company passwords are never stored.

What leaves your network, and only if you turn it on

  • Sign in with Google: Google tells this CasaTorch your Google account ID, e-mail and name when you choose it. Google's privacy policy applies.
  • AI assistant and weekly report: your question and a short summary of your home's current state - and, for the weekly report, the week's energy figures, alert titles and device names - go to the AI provider you choose (for example Anthropic or OpenAI), under their terms; or to a model on your own network. Without an AI connected, the report is written on your CasaTorch.
  • Weather, news, sports, calendars: CasaTorch fetches these from their providers; weather uses your home's approximate location.
  • Security Center: the list of devices on your home network (their network addresses, hardware addresses and names) and the open-port check results are kept on your CasaTorch only. The check looks only at your home network.
  • Secure address at home (on by default; an owner can turn it off in Settings): casatorch.com lists a random name for your CasaTorch and its home-network address (like 192.168.1.20) in public DNS, and Let's Encrypt issues a certificate for that name, which is recorded in public certificate logs. Neither includes your name, e-mail or anything about your home; the address can't be reached from the internet.
  • Cisco Meraki (if you connect it): CasaTorch reads your devices' status from Meraki's online service with your API key.
  • Pro license checks happen offline - the key is verified on your hardware.

What CasaTorch sends to casatorch.com (each can be turned off)

  • Update check, once a day: the version number and how CasaTorch was installed (for example "Windows" or "Raspberry Pi"). Nothing else. Settings > Updates.
  • Find my CasaTorch, every 10 minutes: its address on your home network (like 192.168.1.50), a random ID and the version. We keep it for 30 minutes (or until CasaTorch shuts down) and show it only to browsers on the same home internet connection, at casatorch.com/find and casatorch.com/home. Settings > Updates.
  • Pro keys: when you use "Get my key by e-mail", the e-mail address and the code you type; for monthly and yearly plans, the key itself, so a renewed key can be fetched.
  • Automatic updates (Raspberry Pi and Linux): the new version is downloaded from casatorch.com and GitHub, where CasaTorch's releases are published.

When you buy something on casatorch.com, payment is handled by Stripe and our e-mails are sent through Resend; they process what's needed to do that under their own privacy policies. We never see your card details.

Us

We receive personal data only when you buy from us, contact support or use our services (name, e-mail, order and payment details via our payment provider, and what you send us). We use it to provide what you bought and to support you, keep it only as long as needed, and do not sell it. Contact: support@casatorch.com. Depending on where you live you may have rights to access, correct or delete it.

E-mail list

If you join our e-mail list, we keep your e-mail address, when and on which page you signed up, and the country your internet connection is in (from Cloudflare), only to tell you about CasaTorch (for example when the Box goes on sale). Every e-mail has a one-click unsubscribe link, and unsubscribing deletes your address from the list.

California residents

  • Your requests. E-mail support@casatorch.com to ask what personal data we hold about you, or to correct or delete it. We answer within 45 days and won't treat you differently for asking.
  • We don't sell or share personal data for money or for cross-context behavioral advertising, and we don't disclose it to others for their own direct marketing (California Civil Code 1798.83, "Shine the Light").
  • Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control. casatorch.com doesn't track you across other websites and has no advertising or analytics trackers, so these signals don't change anything here - there is nothing to switch off.
  • CasaTorch is a small business and is below the thresholds of the California Consumer Privacy Act today; we follow the practices above anyway and will update this notice if that changes.

Cameras and other people

If your cameras or sensors can see or record other people, you are responsible for following the privacy laws that apply where you live.

Children

CasaTorch accounts are created by the home's owner. Owners should only give accounts to children in line with the laws that apply to them.

Changes

If this notice changes, the new version is shown when you next sign in.
